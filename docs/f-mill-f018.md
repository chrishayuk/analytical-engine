## 6. What this shows and what it does not

- **Shown, on these two sheets only:** reading the stepped shafts as wheels giving off to zero and receiving tooth for tooth, "Step up" as one cage higher (Vol II p 42), and "Carry" as a warning acted on in row 13, the wheel states reproduce the five products Babbage wrote after band 1 (four from the marks alone, the fifth only when band 6's words are executed), and the multiplicand is retained in every band. The state checks also hold: each band's marked giving wheel is the one holding the number, and "Add to N.<sup>os</sup> one" always finds the product on N.<sup>o</sup> 1.
- **Not shown:** that this is how the engine of January 1836 worked. The rules were written knowing the answers; the engagements are read from Babbage's words and the stepped shafts, which already say at the level of numbers what each band does.
- **Missing, not simulated:** M1 (how the multiplier's figure fixes the number of additions: the simulator takes the order of bands from the chart); M2 (how the B/U bolting and the lifts of the Ver columns put wheels into gear; nothing here uses them); M3 ("Carriage 'b … Zeros"; how a warning is held); M4 (the roles of R2, pp, ap, g, g1, the ′ and ″ parts).
- **Band 6** is executed from its words, not its marks. Under the marks alone the last product fails.
- **Assumed:** six cages on A and C; every wheel the sheet does not mention starts at zero; cage *i* of the multiplicand gives to cage *i* of the product.

A test would apply these rules, unchanged, to a sheet whose numbers have not been read: see the division sheets, whose numbers are already transcribed (`sources/notes/F021-F033-arrows.md`), so that test would not be blind either.
