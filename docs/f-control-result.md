# A provisional controller: the multiplier sets the bands

Generated by `python3 scripts/f_control.py --md docs/f-control-result.md`. **A computational hypothesis**: it shows that a counting mechanism of the kind Babbage describes in December 1835 – March 1836 can generate the sequence of bands, the bolting and the gearing of F/018–019 from the multiplier alone. It does not show that Babbage's engine of January 1836 did so, or which part did the counting.

## 1. Three claims, kept apart

| Claim | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A counter can generate the observed sequence | **shown here, computationally** | sections 3–5 |
| Babbage intended a counter for this | **documented later than F/018, for related designs; not on these sheets** | Vol II p 24 (5 Dec 1835, multiplication by table): "Step up multiplier which reduces its first figure to zero and sets lifters"; Vol II p 68 (about 20 Feb 1836): "Set register to … fig. of A connect it with the lifters and reduce it to zero by stepping down", and a wheel of p − d teeth, "at each tooth step up product"; Vol II p 84 (7 March 1836): a count "put on an axis having a carriage", "a register axis subtr.g unity previous each operation", "a running up will take place at end and thus the op.n be suspended"; Vol XIII history (24 March 1836): an apparatus counting the digits p. Read in `sources/notes/bolting-plates-sources.md` §3.5 and `repetition-count-1836-1842.md` §1–2 |
| R2 is that counter | **not claimed** | see section 6 |

## 2. The mechanism, as run

- **K**, one counting wheel. When the multiplier steps up a cage, its top figure leaves the axis tooth by tooth (rule T4) and each tooth turns K on one tooth: the figure is *given off* onto K.
- **Before each addition**, K is turned back one tooth ("subtracting unity previous each operation"). If it passes from 0 to 9, that is a **running up**: the addition is suspended, notice is given of stepping up, and K is turned on one tooth to stand at zero again (this restoring is assumed).
- **P**, a counter of places, set to the number of figures in the multiplier as written, and turned back one tooth before each step (cage by cage, with borrow). Its running up ends the multiplication. How the engine would obtain p is **missing** (the digit-counting apparatus of February–March 1836 is not reconstructed): p is supplied with the operand.
- **The first step** moves only the multiplier (F/018 band 1, F/020 block 1); later steps move the product and the multiplier together.
- **Each band's bolting** is generated: A Ver to 5 or 6 by which wheel holds the multiplicand; a step's Ver to 5 or 6 and S1 or SS by which wheel holds that number; ′p′p (to 3) or ′P (to 4) by which wheel holds the product. Which wheel holds what is the controller's own state, flipped by each band; it never reads a figure.
- **The gearing** comes from the bolting by E0–E2 (`f-engage-rules.md`) and E3 (exploratory: ′p′p means the product's N.<sup>o</sup> 1 receives, ′P its N.<sup>o</sup> 2; fitted on F/018 and F/020). **The tooth turns** follow T1–T6 (`f-mill-rules.md`).
- **Not generated:** the bolting of P, pp, ap, g, g1, R2, ′g1, 02, ′g2/′g3, 03 and the rest. They have no role in the wheel model.

## 4. F/020, "Bolting plates / Multiplication without Table"

F/020 has no operands. The controller was run on 1028 × 631, a multiplier chosen to give six additions, then three, then at least one, as F/020's blocks do; it is not assumed that F/020 is that run.

**11 of 13 operations agree.** The divergence is block 3: F/020 writes its second step exactly as its first ("Step up from one to two" for the product, "two to one" for the multiplier, the product's Ver bolted to 5 again), and then adds to N.<sup>os</sup> two again. In a continuous run the product would be on N.<sup>o</sup> 2 after the first step, so the second step would go from two to one and the next addition to N.<sup>os</sup> one, as F/019 bands 4–6 do. Block 3's step was re-read on the photograph (216/278) and is written as transcribed. Either F/020 is a schedule of patterns rather than one run, or its block 3 repeats block 2 in error; the sheet does not say.

## 5a. Proved, for every operand

`formal/Babbage/Mill.lean` (Lean 4, no Mathlib, no `sorry`, only the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound) proves the controller's arithmetic at the level of tooth turns, for all operands within capacity: `give_off_retain` (nine rows leave the giver at zero and the retaining wheel holding its figures), `add_band` (give-off plus carriage adds exactly, if the sum fits the cages), `step_band` and `step_multiplier` (one cage higher is ×10, the top figure goes onto K), `countDown_spec` and `add_loop` (K set to d gives exactly d additions, then runs up), and `multiply_correct` / `multiply_lists` (the controller ends, the product wheel holds A × M, the multiplicand is retained, and the bands number p + ΣM), with `multiply_invariant` (after i figures the product is A × the first i figures).

**What the proof does not cover.** Rows and carriage rounds are defined cage by cage, not in `execute`'s loop order; the equivalence is argued in the module's docstring, not proved. The bolting and the rules E0–E3 are not modelled: the proof names the giving and receiving wheels directly. No run compares the Lean definitions with the Python on the same operands. It proves the controller correct, not that it is Babbage's.

## 6. R2

R2 heads a column of the multiplier group. On F/018–019 it is bolted (B3, B4, B3, with lifts 1.1, .6, 1.1) for exactly the three bands in which the multiplier steps, and unbolted in every addition band. That fits a part concerned with the stepping of the multiplier, for instance with receiving its top figure. It does not fit a part that counts the additions by moving: R2 shows no motion in any addition band, and a counter on these sheets would have to be turned back once in each. Nothing here identifies the counter with R2 or with any column.

## 7. Missing, and assumed

- **Missing:** how p, the number of the multiplier's figures, is obtained; how a running up suspends an operation and gives notice (Vol II p 84 says it does, not how); which column, if any, is K.
- **Assumed:** K restored to zero by one tooth after a running up; the first step moves the multiplier alone; enough cages for the product (multiplicand and multiplier figures, plus one).
- **From the earlier layers, unchanged:** the platform 5/6 rule E1 (6 of 6 on unseen sheets where it predicts), E0 (fails on square root's D-C), E2 (untested on unseen sheets), E3 (fitted), and T1–T6 (fitted on F/018–019).
