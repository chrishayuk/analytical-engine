## Date

None on the sheet. The cover has no date, number or day, unlike every sheet
of August 1837, whose covers carry both; the opened sheet has none either.
The catalogue gives the series as 1836-1840 and does not date this item;
Rojas (2017, Table 3) puts "program 27" first, at "1836", with no reason
given in what was read. Nothing seen here confirms or contradicts 1836.

## The opened sheet (222/620)

Five bands of store columns, ruled, with no operations, no mill, no
numbers of operations. Each column is drawn the same way: at the top a
circle, either with + in it (⊕) or empty (○); under it four figure wheels,
each written 0; under those, between rules, what the column is for. Rows of
dots stand for columns left out ("…").

1. **Powers and products.** x, x², x³, x⁴, … (twice), a⁰ … a³, b⁰ … b³,
   y⁰ … y³, xy, x²y, xy², x³y, x²y², xy³, all ⊕; z⁰ … z³, xz, yz, ○. A first
   column x⁰ is drawn entirely in pencil, its sign "(+)". In pencil above
   the first columns: v2, v3, v4, v5 over x … x⁴, "v7" (or v6) over the dots,
   v8 over the second x.
2. **Circular functions.** sin θ, sin 2θ, sin 3θ (twice; the second sin 3θ
   ○), sin η, sin 2η, sin 3η, cos θ, cos 2θ, cos 3θ (twice), sin α,
   sin(α+β), cos α, cos(α+β), all ⊕ but the one. Then pencil labels without
   columns: ε^(x√−1), ε^(−x√−1), a√−1, x√−1, and two more ε-forms, unclear.
3. **Logarithms.** log x, log(a+x), log((a+x)/(b+x)); cos cos θ, cos cos 2θ,
   cos cos 3θ; all ○. Pencil labels: d/dx x^n (?), d/dx x/y, d/dx √(1−x²),
   d/dx f(x).
4. **Powers of polynomials and coefficients.** (a²−x²)^½, ^(3/2), ^(5/2);
   (b²+2ax+x²)^½, ^⅓, ^¼; (a+bx+cx²+∂x³+ex⁴)^½; √((1−x²)/(1+x²)); n/1,
   n·n+1/1·2, n·n+1·n+2/1·2·3; n/1, n·n−1/1·2, n·n−1·n−2/1·2·3; 1/(1+a²),
   1/(1+4a²), 1/(1+9a²); all ○. In pencil, a column ○ 0 0 0 0 "√−1", two more
   pencil circles, and faint "28", "5" below.
5. **A series in x, a and b.** Twenty columns, all ○, each with a power of b
   beneath it; braces group them by the power of a and then of x: x⁰ (one
   column, b⁰ a⁰ x⁰), x¹ (b⁰; a: b⁰ b¹), x² (b⁰; a: b⁰ b¹; a²: b⁰ b¹ b²), x³
   (b⁰; a: b⁰ b¹; a²: b⁰ b¹ b²; a³: b⁰ … b³), and dots. Below, in ink, the
   series whose coefficients the columns hold, in script capitals with a
   figure over and under each: {𝒜b⁰} + {ℬb⁰ + (ℬ₁⁰b⁰ + ℬ₁¹b¹)a}x + {𝒞b⁰ +
   (𝒞₁⁰b⁰ + 𝒞₁¹b¹)a + (𝒞₂⁰b⁰ + 𝒞₂¹b¹ + 𝒞₂²b²)a²}x² + {𝒟 … (𝒟₃⁰b⁰ + 𝒟₃¹b¹ +
   𝒟₃²b² + 𝒟₃³b³)a³}x³ +, continued faintly in pencil with ℰ.

## Compared with the store of the programs

- **One column, one quantity, and a sign wheel.** Every column here is a
  sign over figure wheels. The programs of 1837 never draw the wheels: they
  head a column with what it starts as (BAB/L/001: "+ax +by +c" over v1 to
  v3; L/019 the equation "aw + bx + cy + dz + e = 0" over v1 to v5) and
  write letters in it. The sign appears there only in the heading's "+" and
  in values such as −c − ax, not as a wheel of its own.
- **Signs set and unset.** Bands 1 and 2 have the sign wheel marked + for
  powers, products, sines and cosines; band 1's z-powers and bands 3 to 5
  leave it empty. The sheet does not say why. The engine's notation has no
  sign wheel: a column holds a signed value, and nothing records a column's
  sign separately from its figures, or a sign left unset.
- **Four figure wheels drawn.** Each column shows four 0s; the cover's
  pencil works with numbers of 30 figures, split into two (A = 'a 10³⁰ + a'').
  L/020's cover (1840) says in pencil that "a product as ab requires three
  variables … for it may consist of 80 figures divided on three variables viz
  Head middle & tail". Neither the four drawn wheels nor 30 figures appear on
  the August 1837 sheets, which work in letters, or on the carriage sheets
  (L/008 to L/010), which use three figures.
- **Variable numbers.** Only band 1 has v-numbers, in pencil (v2 … v8),
  skipping v1 for x⁰ and v6 or v7 for the dots. The programs number every
  column v1, v2, ….
- **What the columns are for.** The programs lay out the store for one
  calculation. This sheet lists the kinds of quantity a store would hold for
  whole classes of calculation: series in several letters, trigonometric and
  logarithmic functions, binomial coefficients, roots of polynomials. Band 5
  lays a three-letter series out column by column in order of the powers,
  which is how L/013 and L/014 lay out polynomial coefficients A0, A1, … in
  successive columns.

## Interpretation, not on the sheet

The cover's head-and-tail product reads as an early working of what L/020's
pencil states in 1840: numbers longer than a column, split over two or
three. That the sheet comes before the programs (1836) is Rojas's dating;
the sheet itself does not show it.
