# BAB/F/001 — "Multiplication before the invention of multiplication by table", sheet 1 of 2

**No person has checked this transcription.** It was made by a model from
the images listed below. Read every mark against the images before relying on
it. It is a pilot: only the cover, the column heads, the three partial rows at
the top, and the first complete cycle (rows 1–13) are transcribed.

Three kinds of statement are kept apart throughout:

- **Reading**: what is on the paper (archival reading).
- **Source**: what a cited publication says a sign means, with page.
- **Inference**: this note's own guess. Each one is labelled. None of them is
  used to make the sheet "work".

Uncertain readings are marked `[?x]`. Illegible text is marked `[illeg.]`.

## 4. What the sources say the marks mean

Sources read for this note:

- **[B1826]** Babbage, "On a Method of Expressing by Signs the Action of
  Machinery", *Phil. Trans.* 116 (1826), pp. 250–265. Read from the
  archive.org OCR text (`philtrans09445034`). The plates are not legible in
  the OCR.
- **[S2020]** Swade, *Charles Babbage's Difference Engine No. 2: Technical
  Description* (Science Museum, 2020). Read: pp. 6–8 ("The Mechanical
  Notation") and pp. 20–22 ("Timing and Phasing"). It describes the 1848 DE2
  timing diagram BAB/F/385/1, not this sheet.
- **[Br1982]** Bromley, "Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine, 1838",
  *Annals of the History of Computing* 4(3) (1982). Read: pp. 199–204 and
  p. 207 (the key to Fig. 9). It describes the 1838 design and Bromley's own
  redrawn diagram "similar in style to some of Babbage's own notations".

None of the three describes BAB/F/001 or any 1835 sheet. Every "source"
meaning below therefore comes from a different date or machine. Whether it
applies here is not established.

**Inference (not from a source):** the hatched marks look like cancellations.
The same hatching strikes out the head "Ver. Mo." of c03 and the "B" of c04,
and in the top rows it strikes an "IF". This would mean c03 was abandoned. It
is a guess; nothing on the sheet says so.

## 6. What the rows of cycle 1 seem to do, and what is missing

**As far as the sheet's own words go (reading):**

- Row 2 has the multiplier's digits 1, 0, 2, 1 on the wheels for "n" to
  "n–3", and 9, 0, 8, 9 on the wheels for the "comp.<sup>ts</sup>".
- In row 7 the feeder of the 4th figure is "lowered upon 1."
- "Gears with n." is written for P in rows 5–7.
- In rows 9–10 the Feeder "Feels upon n.".
- In row 10, Y is "Put out." and U is "Put in.".
- In rows 10–13 the lower multiplying pinions n to n–3 each "Gears with" n to
  n–3, with downward arrows in their vertical-motion columns.
- In row 12, F has "Carry" and the cam for lowering the Feeder is "Inactive.".
- In row 13, C<sup>1</sup> is "In gear." (c06), and the other C<sup>1</sup>
  column (c52) has a down arrow with ".5".

**What the sources add.** If the 1848 and 1838 conventions applied (not
established):

- a long arrow's length would be the duration of that motion (S2020 p. 22;
  Br1982 p. 207);
- ".5" would be a lift or lowering of half an inch (S2020 p. 22);
- the dashed arrow in "Single tooth…" would be a conditional motion
  (S2020 p. 22; Br1982 p. 207).

No source explains "IF", the hatched marks, the "Y" mark, or the double
arrows. The sources give no basis for saying what any row does to the numbers
a, b, c, d.

**Missing dependencies for any simulation:**

1. **The drawing of the multiplying pinions** the cover refers to. Its number
   was never filled in ("N.<sup>o</sup> [struck word]"), so it has to be
   found in Series A by date and subject.
2. **The 1835 mill drawings** for every part named in the heads: axes A, B,
   F, C<sup>1</sup>, S, X, Y, U, P; feeders; the gathering-up tooth; the cams;
   the starting teeth; the upper and lower central wheels; the "Figure wheels
   for Product"; the registering tooth; and the multiplier and complement
   wheels. These are needed for gear ratios, the meaning of up and down, and
   which parts mesh with which.
3. **A key to the 1835 signs**, with the "IF" glyph, the "Y" mark, converging
   and diverging double arrows, hatched marks and stepped broken shafts as the
   priorities. None was found in the published sources. Possible places:
   Babbage's own notation rules (the "Buxton papers" rules reported by Swade,
   Plan 28 blog, autumn 2015), Robinson's unpublished account of the notation,
   and Bromley's 1980 Sydney reports 166 and 167.
4. **What "n" denotes** (presumably a cage or digit position, but not stated
   on the sheet), and how n…n–6 map onto cages.
5. **The initial state**: where a, b, c, d stand, and where the product is
   meant to accumulate.
6. **The rest of this sheet**: the later cycles (cycle 2 already differs, for
   example digits "0 0 2 1" in row 2 and "lowered upon 0.") and **BAB/F/002**
   (sheet 2 of 2; images 216/303–305).
7. **The time unit and cycle length** for 1835: whether a band of 13 rows is
   one cycle of the machine, and what the partial rows 11–13 above the first
   band stand for.
8. **The two C<sup>1</sup> columns** (c06 and c52): whether they are the same
   part. Nothing on the sheet says.
