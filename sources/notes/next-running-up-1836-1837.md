## 1. Q1: the quantity whose "next running up" ends the negative cycle

### (a) Vol II p 94, 22 March 1836 (323/6, full resolution)

> At No 4 throw A into gear
> If in 5 q−p runs up A will be moved and cycle Neg. be put in gear. If it
> do not run up then A must be thrown out of gear and B into gear.
> The 1st vert.l of Cycle Neg. puts out A and puts in C which C on the
> next running up puts out the cycle Neg

**The page does not name the quantity.** The working at its head is an
ordinary division of 514 by 3 against a table of multiples of 3 (03 … 27):
"Div.d 000514 / 03 / 21" (05 − 03 = 2, the 1 brought down: 21); "051 / 03 /
021 4 / 21[?4, overwritten] / 210 / 004" (51 − 30 = 21; 214 − 210 = 4). It
shows nothing about the negative cycle or the wheels it changes.

### (a) p 95

> The term.n of Pos. or of Neg. Cyc acts jointly with the term.n of table
> making to put in the real division cycle

The square-root lines below ("·000021 / ·000010 [struck] /
0·000000000021", "−(p+q)/2" or "(−p+q)/2", "30 − q+1 + d[?]") and the wheels
A, B, C with every other tooth cut concern counting digits for a root.
They are a different A, B, C from p 94's barrels.

### (a) p 78, 1 March (from `running-up-designs-1836.md` §5)

The dividend is stepped "up or down … q−p times", and a stop at the end of
this, "in concurrence with" the stop at the end of table making, "throws
into gear division cycles". The same structure as p 95.

### (a) p 90, 12 March (full resolution)

"0019 / 9981 / 19 / 0000 / 1 / 1 / 0002 / p−q", "21 = 19+2": a number set
to −19 (9981) is counted up through zero, and what is counted beyond zero
is the difference.

### (a) pp 96–101, 23–31 March (overview; labels at full resolution)

Drawings only: p 96 (23 March) a counting apparatus with "p", "q",
"units", "tens wh."; p 99 (24 March) racks marked 29, 20/19, 10/9; p 101
(30 March) "p / q / q−p / q+d−p", bars "q−p" and "d + q−p" with zero marks.
Nothing on the negative or positive cycle.

### (a) p 102, 3 April 1836 (full resolution; checked again)

> p<q step Div. down q−p times and Subtr.
> Step Div.d up p−q times

A drawn "rack for q−p", with "q−p>0" and "q−p<0" on its two rows, teeth or
pins N, M, L:

> If q > p rack in backing throws into gear single tooth L which advances
> it untill q−p is gath.d up when it puts stepping out of gear by tooth N.
> If q < p rack in advancing throws into gear pin M which gathers up
> [?p−q] teeth and then by tooth N throws stepping out of gear

and, under a further rack, "Rack to throw ~~out~~ in Dividing cycle", with
"+7"; "In the 13 the 1st [?subtr.n] is made / In the 6 the 1st stepping is
made".

### (a) p 103

"Racks A & B are thrown into gear so that" (unfinished), A "q−p", B "+7";
"q−5 / 7+(q−5) / 7+(q−5)−p".

### (b) What it means, and the candidates kept apart

| Candidate | For | Against | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) a count fixed before the loop starts, set from the computed difference q − p** (the negative cycle steps the dividend \|q − p\| times, ended when that count is used up) | p 95 puts the end of either cycle with the end of table making, before "the real division cycle"; p 78 (1 March) steps the dividend "q−p times" in the same structure; p 102 (3 April) ends the stepping when "q−p is gath.d up", in either direction | p 102 is twelve days later and ends the stepping by a rack and "tooth N", not by a running up; p 94 itself does not say | **favoured, as an interpretation** |
| **(b) a number computed during the negative cycle**, such as a remainder | — | p 95 places the end of the negative cycle before division proper; nothing found where a quantity changed inside the cycle runs up | **no support found** |
| **(c) some other mechanical state** | — | — | **not excluded** |

**Verdict: the pages do not settle it.** If (a) is right, the 22 March
design is a choice on the sign of a computed difference, followed by a
repetition whose count is that difference, fixed before the repetition
starts. It would then add branching on a computed result, but not a loop
that ends on a number computed inside it.

### (a) p 145 (same image, right page; same depth)

> Division with one set of picking up sectors
> such as those on Drawing N.o 52
> q = N.o of digits in dividend p = ditto in divisor d = N.o of decimals

then four bracketed cases, each "Not.n 71" against "Method described below":
"q+1−p / 9+1−14 = −4 expressed by 996" and "p−q−1 / 14−9−1 = +4 expressed by
4" → "Step <u>up</u> 4 times"; "q+1−p+d / 9+1−14+8 = +4 expressed by 4" and
"p−q−d−1 / 14−9−8−1 = −4 expressed by 996" → "4 figures in quotient";
"q+1−p / 8+1−5 = +4 exp.d by 4" and "p−q−1 / 5−8−1 = −4 exp.d by 996" →
"Step <u>down</u> 4 times"; "q+1−p+d / 8+1−5+3 = +7 exp.d by 7" and "p−q−d−1
/ 5−8−3−1 = −7 exp.d by 993" → "7 figures in quotient"; and

> In the method described below (or rather in next page) when p−q−d−1 = 0
> or +1 or +2 &c there are no figures in quotient

### (b) pp 144–145

- p 144 counts by transferring: each unit counted off counting apparatus
  No 2 is added to No 1, and when No 2 reaches 0, No 1's sign is changed
  and d subtracted. A count is turned into a signed number on wheels.
- "The highest figure of the Mult.r is used first. The multiplier is stepped
  up and the Table is stepped down": the order of multiplication, highest
  figure first, the multiplier stepped up. This is multiplication **by
  table**, 13 Nov 1837; it is the order the provisional controller of
  `docs/f-control-result.md` uses for multiplication without table
  (January 1836), but it is not evidence for that earlier design.
- p 145: digit counts of dividend and divisor are combined into signed
  counts written in complement on three wheels (−4 as 996, −7 as 993), and
  the **sign** of the count decides "Step <u>up</u>" or "Step <u>down</u>",
  and how many figures the quotient has; when p−q−d−1 is 0 or positive,
  "there are no figures in quotient". So in November 1837, in the mill, a
  count computed from the operands is held as a signed number and its sign
  chooses the direction of stepping. No running up is named on these two
  pages.

p 147's three-way order ("If it runs up order Stepping down … if it now
runs up order no stepping but if it does not run up order Stepping up") is
still known only from the earlier preview reading. pp 146–155 not yet read
at full resolution.
