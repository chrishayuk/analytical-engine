# BAB/F/018–020 — "Multiplication without table", 11 January 1836, and "Bolting plates to multiply without table"

**No person has checked this transcription.** It was made by a model from
the images listed below. Read every mark against the images before relying
on it. It covers the covers of all three sheets, both tables of F/018–019
(six bands of 16 rows and the partial rows above each sheet's first band)
and the whole table of F/020.

Conventions as in [`F001.md`](F001.md): **Reading** (what is on the paper),
**Source** (what a cited publication says, with page), **Inference** or
**HYPOTHESIS** (this note's own, labelled; none is used to make the sheets
"work"). `[?x]` is an uncertain reading, `[illeg.]` illegible. New marks are
numbered S40 onward (`../../docs/f-concordance.md` section 5), to keep clear
of numbers the BAB/F/013 transcription may add.

The primes on the product heads ('P, 'p'p, 'b, 'g1, 'S'S …) and the double
primes on the multiplier's ("S"S, "S1) set the three groups apart; the
multiplicand's heads have none. That reading of the accents' purpose is an
inference.

**No head names a snail, feeler, feeder, gathering-up tooth, registering
tooth, multiplying pinion, central wheel, or complement.** None of F/001's
part names recurs. Only the letters A and P, and the word "Carry", are
shared, and sharing a letter does not show the same part. So neither of the
two methods F/002's notes name ("Snails and feelers … ten angular
positions", c07–c14; "a feeler which has only two angular positions",
c36–c52) appears on these sheets **by name or by column**.

**Row numbers (reading).** Each band has 16 rows, about 106 px each. The
row-number column carries two numbers per row: on the right, in darker
ink, 1–16; on the left, in lighter ink, 2, 3, … 15 beside right-hand 1–14,
then a small "⅓" in the upper part of row 15, "⅔" beside right-hand 15, and
"1" beside right-hand 16. The same on both sheets and in every band. What
the left numbering counts is not said (HYPOTHESIS: the time of another
cycle offset by one unit, with unit 15 divided in thirds; nothing supports
it but the layout). On 273 a ruled vertical line runs through the
right-hand numbers.

## 5. What the sources say

As `F001.md` section 4; nothing found describes these sheets.

- ".4", "1.1", ".45" beside short arrows: **Source** S2020 p. 22 (1848):
  numbers beside short vertical arrows give the displacement in inches.
  Form matches; date does not.
- Double arrowheads (S40): **Source** Br1982 p. 207 (1838): "double
  arrowheads indicate a return to the inactive position".
- "Carry", "Zeros", "Figs", "Step up", "Give off and retain", "Add to
  N.<sup>os</sup> one": Babbage's words.

Columns in motion (arrows and stepped shafts) by time unit 1–16:
cycle 1: 3 in units 1–9, 3 in 11, 8 in 16; cycles 2–3: 4 in 1–9, 5 in 11,
1 in 13, 3 in 14–15, 8 or 10 in 16; cycles 4–5: 6 in 1–9, 6 in 11, 4 in

7 in 16. Units 10 and 12 have no arrow in any cycle.

**Observations needing no meaning for the marks:**

1. **The multiplier's figures are never written.** Unlike F/001–002 (1021,
   0021, … 0000 in row 2), there is nothing to count down. How the machine
   knows the figure 2 means two additions is not on the sheet.
2. **The product changes in two ways only.** It grows by exactly 1028 in
   the cycles with "Add to N.<sup>os</sup> one" (2, 3, 6), and is multiplied
   by 10, its figures moving one place, in the cycles with "Step up" in the
   product group (4, 5). In cycle 1 only the multiplier group steps; the
   product is 000000 before and after.
3. **Additions: two, then shifts: two, then one addition.** With
   201 = 2·100 + 0·10 + 1, the order on the sheet is: (cycle 1) multiplier
   stepped; (2, 3) add, add; (4) product and multiplier stepped; (5)
   product and multiplier stepped; (6) add. The product's figures confirm
   the arithmetic of each step (0 → 1028 → 2056 → 20560 → 205600 →
   206628).
4. **A ↑ with a figure in row 16 is always followed, in the next band, by a
   ↓↓ in the same column, with the same figure where one is written** (row
   11, or rows 14–15 in e21, e22, e24, e25). Example: cycle 1 row 16 e01 ↑
   ".4", e05 ↑ "1.1", e08 ↑ ".6" → cycle 2 row 11 e01 ↓↓ ".4", e05 ↓↓ "1.1",
   e08 ↓↓ ".6". It holds for all six cycles and both partial rows (cycle 3's
   e34 "6" is matched by cycle 4's ↓↓ ".6", so "6" is probably ".6" with the
   point not written or not seen).
5. **Each "U n" follows a "B n" in the same column.** Cycle 1 e01 B5 → cycle
   2 e01 U5; e05 B3 → U3; e08 B4 → U4; top e34 B3 → cycle 1 U3; e35 B5 → U5.
   "B" labels stand over the row-16 ↑; "U" labels have no arrow.
6. **The two figure wheels of an axis alternate.** In every stepping or
   give-off cycle one of N.<sup>o</sup>1/N.<sup>o</sup>2 has a stepped shaft
   down with "Zeros" and the other a stepped shaft up with "Figs", and the
   roles swap on the next use of that axis (A: cycle 2 N.<sup>o</sup>1
   Zeros, cycle 3 Figs; B: cycle 4 N.<sup>o</sup>1 Zeros, cycle 5 Figs; C:
   cycles 1, 4, 5 alternate). The words "one to two" and "two to one", and
   F/020's "Give off from N.<sup>o</sup> one Retain on N.<sup>o</sup> two" and
   its converse, name the same alternation.

**HYPOTHESES (not adopted).**
- H-A (Horner order): the machine multiplies by the highest figure of the
  multiplier first, adding the multiplicand once per unit of that figure,
  then shifts the product one place ("Step up") for each later figure before
  adding for it. *For:* observation 3 and the product figures, which show it
  directly. *Against / open:* the 0 figure costs a step but no add; cycle 1
  steps the multiplier with nothing added; why cycle 5 steps the multiplier
  again and cycle 6 does not is not shown.
- H-B (two wheels per axis, giving off and retaining): "Give off and retain"
  copies a number from one figure wheel of an axis to the other while
  keeping it, so that 1028 stays on A (e13 reads 1028 in every cycle).
  *For:* observation 6 and F/020's wording. *Against:* no drawing.
- H-C (B/U = bolt/unbolt): "B n" is a numbered plate bolted (with the row-16
  ↑ as its motion), "U n" the same plate unbolted, and ↓↓ the return. *For:*
  observations 4 and 5; F/020's title; Br1982 p. 207 for double heads.
  *Against:* nothing on the sheets defines B or U; some ↑ have no label.

**Snail and feeler methods.** F/002's notes assign c07–c14 to the snail
(ten-position) method and c36–c52 to the two-position feeler. On F/018–019
**neither group's parts appear**, so neither method can be said to appear.
What the sheet does show is a figure (2) taking two addition cycles, which
is closer to F/001–002's count-down by units (one cycle for each unit) than
to multiplying by a whole figure at once; but the mechanism that counts is
not drawn.

**Revision or the same design redrawn?** Not the same design redrawn: the
parts, column heads, cycle length, symbols, and the way the place changes
(product stepped, not pinions re-geared) all differ, and the test numbers
differ. **HYPOTHESIS:** it is a later design of the same operation, made
after "Table multiplication" (F/013, 5 January 1836) and sharing its
vocabulary rather than F/001's: F/013's preview (`../downloads/smg/F/F013/prev/259L.jpg`,
looked at only for this comparison, not transcribed here) has the same
grouping by axis with "Ver"/"Cir", "Figure wheels N.<sup>o</sup> 1 …",
"Zeros"/"Figures" under stepped shafts, B/U labels with figures over
arrows, "Carry", and the phrases "Receive Addition upon N.<sup>o</sup> one"
and "Step down Product from one to two". Its cycle has 15 rows with a
"2/3" row, against 16 here. The F/013 transcription should settle how
close the two are.
