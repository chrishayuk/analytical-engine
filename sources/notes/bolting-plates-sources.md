# "Bolting plates", "platforms" and the B/U labels: archival sources

Read by a coding agent from the Science Museum Group images, November 2026.
**No person has checked these readings.** This note looks for what Babbage
meant by "bolting plates", "platforms" and the labels "B n" / "U n" of the
multiplication sheets of January 1836 (F/013, F/014, F/018–019, F/020; see
`docs/f-concordance.md` S41, `docs/f-cycles.md` "The bolting-plate sheets",
`docs/f-bu-audit.md`). It gives each source as **Observation** (what is on
the paper), **Reading** (what the words or marks say, read together), and
**Hypothesis** (what it might mean for B/U). Nothing here is a claim of
novelty, and no meaning is adopted into the data.

Method: as in `sketchbooks-control.md`. Every page of Vol II from the
fly-leaf to p 71 was first read from the 1536 px previews
(`coimages … large_000000NN.jpg`). Every passage quoted below was then read
at full resolution from aligned 512 px tiles of the zoom image, stitched
locally. Regions are given as x, y, w, h in full-resolution pixels of the
zoom image. Previews and crops were saved in the session scratchpad only and
are not kept in the repository; the regions are given so that they can be
fetched again. Conventions: `[?word]` doubtful, `~~struck~~`,
`⟨interlined⟩`, `.n` for a raised ending.

Vol II = BAB/S/1/02, "Scribbling Book Volume II" (`aa110001323`), zoom
files `322/957`… (image 1) to `323/…`, named
`Scribbling_Book_Volume_II__1835_November_1837_February.ptif`. Images are
two-page spreads, about 4880 × 2950 px. Babbage numbers only the odd
(right-hand) pages; the even page numbers below are inferred.

## 1. Summary

- **No source found defines "B", "U" or "bolting plates" in so many words.**
  Nothing found says "B means bolted".
- **"Platforms" is defined by use.** From 30 November 1835 to 20 February
  1836, Vol II has "travelling platforms". They are raised, lowered and
  brought to a "neutral point" in every cycle. Axes, pinions and wheels are
  "locked" to them or "unlocked" from them, by number ("Pl. 3", "platform
  N.o 1"). Drawing BAB/A/029 (4 Feb 1836) is titled **"Method of bolting to
  platforms for vertical motion of Axes"**, and its label reads "Bolting
  Axes to Platforms for Vert. Mot.n".
- **Vol II pp 42–43 (21–23 Dec 1835) is the nearest thing to a key.** It is a
  schedule for multiplication from a table, written as words: "Lock — centre
  pinion — carr. pin.s — on platform N.o 1", "Lock — Step. Ax. … to Pl. 3",
  "Unlock — Cen. Pin from Pl. … Pl. 3", "Lock — the 2 centre pin.ns to
  Pl. 4". It has the same form as F/013's labels: a named part, a verb, and
  a numbered platform. The verb there is "lock", not "bolt".
- **The strongest internal evidence is already in `F013.md` §5.1.** Where an
  F/013 label reads "B k", the arrow under it carries the displacement
  written in the head of **platform N.o k**, in 14 of 15 cases. Together
  with p 42 and A/029, this supports a refined hypothesis:

  > **H′.** "B k" written in a part's column = that part bolted to
  > (travelling) platform k. "U k" = unbolted from platform k. The figure k
  > names a platform, which several parts may share at once.

- **Against H′, or not explained by it.** Babbage's own word in December
  1835 is "lock/unlock". "U" is also used on F/013 with "L" in the three
  "Lock" columns (t28–t30), where it can only be "unlock". The titles of F/014
  and F/020 say "plates", not "platforms". The figureless "B" and "B′", and
  the B's never followed by a U, stay unexplained. Vol II p 55 (Jan 1836)
  has a bare "B U B, B U. B" whose meaning is unknown.
- **The platform scheme was short-lived.** On 20 Feb 1836, Vol II p 69 says
  "Decided that tables should be made by means of the ordinary sectors and
  not by means of platforms". BAB/A/030–031 (27 Feb 1836) are "sectors for
  giving vertical motion to axes".

## 2. History sheets (Series N)

### 2.1 BAB/N/002, History sheet for 1835 (`223/685`)

Observation (full resolution, region 5000,1330,1350,420; confirms `N002.md`):

> Barrels to bolt axes to travelling platforms, Drums to govern Barrels

The cell is "Barrels Drums & Cards", in the row **Feb. 1835**. Region
5650,1690,700,350, row Mar. 1835, "Miscellaneous Contrivances":

> Locking plates to moveable levers

Reading. In February 1835 the verb "bolt" is already joined to "axes" and
"travelling platforms", and barrels govern the bolting. "Plates" in March
1835 belong to a locking part ("locking plates"), not to platforms.

Hypothesis. This makes "bolt (an axis) to a travelling platform" Babbage's
phrase about ten months before F/013. It is also evidence that "plate" and
"platform" were distinct words for him (see §6, claim 5).

Not re-read. `N003.md` has no entry with bolt, plate or platform. Its Feb
1836 "Count.g App. for Mult.n … 29" has the heavy number 29. That is
BAB/A/029, the bolting drawing (`references.md` already notes the mismatch
of subject).

## 3. Scribbling Book Vol II (BAB/S/1/02), 6 Nov 1835 – 24 Feb 1836

### 3.1 p 7 (image 4, `322/960`), Nov 1835 (between p 5, 6 Nov, and p 8, 24 Nov)

Observation (region 2600,100,2228,1300). Top right, two columns:

> Pin.s  1.1 / .6 / −.6 / −1.1      Axis  .4 / .2 / −.2 / −.4

Below a lever sketch: "a : p :: b : (b/a) p = .6", "a+x : p :: b−x : ((b−x)/(a+x)) p = 1.1",

.4 and .2 to "Axis". The proportions look like lever arms giving two lifts

Hypothesis (weak). On F/013 the labels in the two **Axis** "Ver" columns (t01

labels in the pinion/wheel columns name 3, 4, 9 and 10 (±1.1, ±.6). That fits
the p 7 split. It is a pattern noticed in `F013.md` §4, not stated anywhere.

Reading. This is the first use of "travelling platforms" in Vol II. They lie

### 3.3 p 17 (image 9, `322/965`), 1 Dec 1835

Observation (region 2560,130,2300,1050). A cycle table, with figures in turns:

> Add nine or less · 9 · this includes 1/3 for locking centrals

Hypothesis. On F/013 every B/U label and the "U&B" in "Bolt to platforms"
(t43) are written in the row numbered **"2/3"** at the head of each band.
The band ends with a row "1/3", and p 23 also has a "1/3" for "lock all
the wheels". The match of the fractions is a pattern, not a statement.

### 3.5 p 24 (image 13, `322/969`), 5 Dec 1835

Observation (region 180,80,2250,1550):

> Cycle for compound ~~Multiplicat~~ Multiplication / having made table
> Step up multiplier which reduces its first figure to zero and sets lifters } 9
> Lift lifters } 1
> Lower stepping axis lower and giving off axis
> Give off from Table to Product — · 9
> Carry (see page 23) — · 1/3 [beside it: "During 1 of this lift lifters"]
> Lock all
> Travelling Platforms descend to Neutral P.t · 1
> Lock or unlock Trav. Platform · 2/3
> Raise Travelling Platforms · 1 · ~~lift lifters~~

### 3.6 p 39 (image 20, `322/976`), between 17 and 21 Dec 1835

Observation (region 2600,60,2283,900):

> Platform lifting pin.s to lift. axes and returning them up untill after carriage.

Below it are four small sketches of a pin over a stepped block, and "1 add to all
9 and carry / 2 do 8 do / 3 do 7 do / 4 do 6 do". In the margin: "1.1 / .6 /

### 3.7 p 42 (image 22, `322/978`), between 21 and 23 Dec 1835

p 40 is headed "21 Dec" and p 43 "24 Dec". "23 Dec" is written under this
passage, beside a sketch.

Observation (region 380,80,2150,2100). Hand legible, a few figures
overwritten:

> For Mult.n ~~on~~ ⟨of A⟩ N.o [blotted figure] Mill
> Receive from table
> Lock — centre pinion ⟨to N.o 3⟩ — carr. pin.s — on platform N.o 1
> [pencil, faint, at left: "Add to [?product]"]
> For Stepping up
> Lock — ~~plat~~ Step. Ax. [?S] [small "2" above] to Pl. 3 — Ad. Ax. to [?Pl. 5, overwritten]
> Unlock — Cen. Pin from Pl. [figure struck or overwritten] Pl. 3
> Product is now on A 2 ⟨but⟩ on a cage higher
> A1 is to rec. another from table
> Lock — the 2 centre pin.ns to Pl. [?4, written over another figure] — Carr. pins to N.o 2
> For Step up from N.o 2 to N.o 1
> Lock — Step. Ax. [?S] to Pl. 4 — A2 to Pl. 6
> Unlock — the 2 cent. pinion from P[?l]. 2

Reading.
- Each step of multiplication from a table is a list of parts locked to,
  or unlocked from, platforms **named by number** (1, 2, 3, 4, 6).
- The parts named (centre pinions, carriage pinions, stepping axis, adding
  axis, A2) are the kinds of part in F/013's heads: "Cen Pin ″P", "Car pin
  ″g2", ″S/′S, "Axis C", "Axis B".
- **Two different parts are locked to platform 4 in one sequence** ("the 2
  centre pin.ns to Pl. 4", then "Step. Ax. … to Pl. 4"). Two parts are also
  locked "to N.o 2"/"Pl. 2" at different times. A platform is a shared
  carrier, not one per part.
- The words "Product is now on A 2 but on a cage higher" give the purpose:
  a part carried by a platform is moved vertically by a cage (a step).

Hypothesis. This is the same notation, in words, as F/013's labels written
in a part's column ("B N.o 3" in t09 ″S″S; "U N.o 3" in t07 "Cen Pin ″P").
"Lock … to Pl. k" corresponds to "B k" and "Unlock … from Pl. k" to "U k".
**Against:** the word is "lock", and the sheets of January 1836 write "B".
If B/U were a direct abbreviation of this page, one would expect "L" (and
F/013 uses "L" for its lock columns). So the letter B points to a later
change of word or of mechanism: "bolt", as in A/029 and N/002.

### 3.8 p 55 (image 28, `322/984`), between 5 and 22 Jan 1836

p 52 is headed "5 Jan 1836" and p 59 "22 Jan".

Observation (region 3400,60,1475,700). At the head of a page of algebra
(p ± q√−1, x² − ax + b = 0), with two sketches of wheels with stops, large:

> B · U B, B U. B

The last B is crossed by an oblique stroke. There are short vertical strokes
above, between the letters; no figures.

Reading. A sequence of the letters B and U, written within days of F/013
(5 Jan) and F/018 (11 Jan). Nothing on the page connects it to platforms.

Hypothesis. Possibly a bolting sequence (bolt, unbolt, bolt …). It could
equally be unrelated. **Not used as evidence for or against.**

### 3.9 p 63 (image 34, `322/990`), 9 Feb 1836

Observation (region 3200,60,1677,300):

> For each fig. wh.
> ~~Give off~~ = Short time
> Receive = Short ~~[?time if]~~
> D.o as part of Mult = Long time

Reading. This is the same vocabulary as F/013's platform groups, "Long

### 3.10 p 69 (image 37, `322/993`), 20 Feb 1836

Observation (region 2560,950,2329,1650):

> 20 Feb.y
> Decided that tables should be made by means of the ordinary sectors and
> not by means of platforms. Nine or ten of the sectors are to be put into
> gear by axes segment or some other mechanical contrivance which is acted
> on by the tooth on the drum ⟨of Mill⟩ which sectors are to be put out one
> by one as ⟨which⟩ the making of the table proceeds.
> When the table is used the lifters are to select two sectors (one of
> Adding axis and one of [?Product] axis) and put them in; after giving off,
> ⟨segment or⟩ something else which acts on all the giving off sectors is to
> put ~~them~~ the one used out of gear and ~~when~~ ⟨after⟩ the number is
> returned the sector of Add.g pin. Ax is to be acted on in the same manner.

Reading. Until 20 Feb 1836, tables were to be made "by means of
platforms". From that date they were to be made by sectors. F/013 (5 Jan,
with a note of 6 Feb 1836) and F/014 belong to the platform period.

### 3.11 Not found in Vol II pp 1–71

- No definition of "B" or "U", and no list of numbered platforms with their
  lifts other than p 7's four figures.
- No word "bolt", "bolting", "unbolt" or "plate" in the sense of the F
  sheets.
- No description of how a part is bolted (the mechanism). The sketches on
  pp 21, 31, 37 and 61 show axes with blocks and slides, but they carry no
  words that tie them to platforms, and they were not read in detail.

  read at full resolution. **The whole sheet was not read at full

Reading.
- This is a drawing whose whole subject is "bolting to platforms" for the
  vertical motion of axes. It is dated four weeks after F/013 and three after
  F/018–020.

the plates or platforms that pass it. If so, "bolting plates" may mean
these platforms in plan, and "B k" means an axis's bolt shot into platform
k. This is an inference from the drawing's form and title, not a reading
of any label.

### 4.3 Later uses of the same words (catalogue titles only, not opened)

These show that "bolt", "platform" and "locking plates" stayed in Babbage's
vocabulary in other senses and designs:

- BAB/A/180/1 (undated; Difference Engine No. 2): "Plan for raising axes
  (vertical motion) … Locking plates and travelling platforms."
- BAB/P/165, P/168 (1859): "Combination of cams, platform and stop bolts.
  Selectors for table", and "… table axis with combination of cams and
  platforms … stop bolts and barrels."
- BAB/P/169 (2 Aug 1859): "Rough draft of mill and driving without
  platforms."
- BAB/P/202 (Dec 1868 – Jan 1869): "three moveable platforms of which the

  in "table making, multiplication, and addition and subtraction".
- BAB/P/207 (1 Aug 1870): "Plan and elevation of bolts for each axis in the
  mill and motion for raising platform."

## 5. Published notation literature

### 5.1 Babbage 1826, "On a Method of Expressing by Signs the Action of Machinery", Phil. Trans. 116, 250–265

Read from the archive.org OCR text (`philtrans09445034`), searched for
"bolt", "plate", "platform", "lock", "click", "valve".

Observation. Bolts occur twice. The first is in the transmission signs:
"One piece may be driven by another, and yet not always move when the
latter moves; as is the case when a stud lifts a bolt once in the course of
its revolution". The second is in the timing rules, p 259–260:

> 9. If the thing indicated be a click, bolt, or valve, its dotted line
> should be on the right side if it is out of action, unbolted, or open,
> and on the left side if the reverse is the case.
> 10. If a bolt may rest in three positions: 1st, bolted on the right side;
> 2nd, unbolted; 3rd, bolted on the left side. When it is unbolted, and in
> the middle station, use two lines whilst in the act of unbolting, …

(OCR, lightly corrected: "ist" → "1st", "srd" → "3rd".) No "platform", and
"plate" only for the engraved plates of the paper.

Reading. "Bolted" and "unbolted" are Babbage's 1826 names for the states of
a bolt. In 1826 the states are shown by the side of a dotted line, **not by
letters**. Nothing in 1826 uses B or U.

### 5.2 Bromley 1982, "Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine, 1838"

Read from the PDF named in `docs/literature-mechanical-notation.md`, as text,
searched for "bolt", "platform", "plate", "lock", "vertical motion",
"raised". No "bolt" and no "platform". "Plates" occurs only for the framing
plates that separate cages. "Lockings" are described (units 13–15 of the
cycle). In the 1838 design a control lever "whose purpose is to raise an
axis will not itself raise it, but will put into gear with the main drive
shaft a cam that raises the axis".

Reading. Bromley's 1838 mechanism for raising axes is a cam per axis, put
into gear by the barrel, not a shared platform. Nothing in Bromley bears on
the 1836 B/U labels.

### 5.3 Swade 2020 (DE2 technical description)

As summarised in `docs/literature-mechanical-notation.md` (not re-read):
locks are shown as states "F" (Free) and "L" (Locked) on BAB/F/385/1
(1848). That is a letter notation for lock states. It has no B or U.

## 6. Evidence against H′, and other senses of "bolt"

1. **"Lock", not "bolt", in Vol II.** Every December 1835 passage (pp 17, 23,
   24, 42) says "lock/unlock … to … platforms". "Bolt" appears with
   platforms only in N/002 (Feb 1835), F/013's head t43 "Bolt to
   platforms", the F/014 and F/020 titles, and A/029 (Feb 1836).
2. **"U" is also "unlock".** On F/013 the three columns headed "Lock"
   (t28–t30) carry "U" (up arrow) and "L" (down arrow) with no figure, every
   band. So "U" alone does not distinguish "unbolt" from "unlock". On
   H′ it is the "un-" of whichever verb the column has. Read that way, the
   figureless U's of t28–t30 are not counterexamples to the B/U pairing:
   they pair with L.
3. **Figureless "B" and "B′".** F/014 ca, ′ca; F/020 02 "B′", 03 "B". These
   are not explained. F/013 writes "ca moves at same time" in the column
   where F/014 has a bare B.
4. **B's never followed by a U** (eight on each sheet, `f-bu-audit.md`). On
   H′ a part may stay bolted past the end of the sheet. Nothing confirms
   this.
5. **"Plates" vs "platforms".** F/014 ("Bolt.g plates", "Bolting plates.
   Multiplication by Table") and F/020 ("Bolting plates to multiply without
   table") say plates. F/013 and A/029 say platforms. N/002 uses "plates"
   for another part ("Locking plates to moveable levers", Mar 1835), and
   A/180/1 names "Locking plates and travelling platforms" side by side.
   So "Bolting plates" could name plates that do the bolting, not the
   platforms. A/029 draws the platforms as plates in plan, which allows
   either reading.
6. **Platform numbers do not agree across sheets.** F/013 has twelve
   platforms; F/014's labels run 1–10; F/018–020's run 1–6 (concordance).
   p 42 uses 1, 2, 3, 4, 6. No source gives the number of platforms for the
   design "without table".
7. **"Bolt" in other senses.** In 1826 it is a click or bolt in or out of
   action. In 1859–1870 there are "stop bolts" and "bolts for each axis"
   (P/165, P/168, P/207). Those are later and are not evidence for 1836.

**A consequence for `docs/f-bu-audit.md`.** Its P4 ("no figure is bolted in
two columns at once") assumes one plate per part. Vol II p 42 locks two
different parts to platform 4 in one sequence. On H′, P4 is therefore not a
prediction, and its failures are not evidence against bolting. They are
what shared travelling platforms would produce. The audit's reading "the
figure is relative to its column" is weakened by F/013 §5.1 (the figure
equals platform k's head figure) and by p 42.
