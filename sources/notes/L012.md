## What the cells imply (interpretation, not on the sheet)

Reading "C.x" as −x (a complement), every store cell of the sheet follows
from three rules:

1. A column read is left at zero, and its number goes, complemented, to
   every column and Car the row names, each of which **adds** it to what it
   holds (row 4: v6 holds C.Δ²M and becomes C.ΔM1 = C.(ΔM0 + Δ²M)).
2. A Car marked "− 0" gives its number to the columns the row names, and is
   cleared; Car1 at rows 5, 10, … gives to nothing written (its sum is
   already in v6 or v14).
3. v1 and v9, which feed the mill, always receive the true M and N: at row 7
   (and 12, 31, 36) from a Car holding a complement (C.M1 → M1), at row 19
   (and 24) from one holding the true number (M2 → M2). One rule for "− 0"
   that complements or one that does not fits rows 5 and 7 together, or 17
   and 19, but not all four; the sheet does not say how v1 gets the true
   number in cycle 1.

The "Car" columns look like the carriages (carry mechanisms) of the store's
additions; that the store adds in parallel with the mill is Rojas's reading
of this program (2017), and the cover's count, ten small operations per P,
counts them.
