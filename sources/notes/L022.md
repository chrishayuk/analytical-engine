the sheet (v3 v2)(v2 v21), the engine (v3 v21), because it derives a

no disagreement in the sheet.

∂ and e from the run satisfy ∂z + e = 0 for the z that solves the three
equations (checked with numbers outside the engine).

## What the engine would need

1. A row that both operates and moves a number: at rows 8 and 17 the
   columns left at zero are the operation's (v5; v10, v25) and the moved
   number's (v3; v8). Without a formula column the engine takes every
   column left at zero as an operand. It could take as operands only the
   columns whose numbers the mill column names, and treat a column read
   whose number goes, through a transit column, to another column as a
   transfer.
2. Cards for a transfer through a complement column: (from, transit),
   (transit, to).

## Compared with No. 20 (31 July 1840)

Both reduce the same three equations, first to γ, δ, ε and 'γ, 'δ, 'ε, and
then to ∂z + e = 0. No. 20 keeps each B in the column of its equation (v2,
v7, v12) with shared complement columns (v16, v17), writes a count of
cards for each row, totals for each group and a running count from No. 19,
and ends with "÷ −e/∂ = z". No. 22 moves the B's to columns of their own
(v21, v23, v25) with a complement column each, names the operands of every
row beside it ("v21 v8"), lists the cards without per-row counts (one
count, 37, after row 9), moves γ and 'γ into the B's columns so that the
second table repeats the first's pattern of cards, and stops at ∂z + e = 0
with no division and no cards for the second table ("&c similar").
