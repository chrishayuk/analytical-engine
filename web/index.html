Babbage's programs of August 1837, run as he wrote them

Two programs on one sheet, BAB/L/001: No. 1 of 4 August 1837 eliminates x between two equations; No. 2 of 5 August finds x and y. Each operation is done on the store and the mill as the sheet sets it out, and every cell Babbage wrote is checked against what the operation does. babbage, compiled to WebAssembly, runs in this page.

What running it found

123 statements of the sheet are checked: every store cell, mill entry and formula, every accent on a variable, both compositions, x and y as stated, and every count of operations and variables. 122 agree.

The one that does not is No. 2's operation 12. It is a subtraction, and the sheet writes its result as −v₃″ − v₅″, that is −c − ax. Neither order of subtracting the two numbers gives that; the mill would have to take both negatively, which no other row asks of it.

Operation 2 of both programs multiplies by b′ although operation 1 has left v5, which held b′, at zero. The mill keeps the table of multiples of b′ it made, and Babbage ordered the products to use it again; his remark under No. 1 rejects a saving of variables because the table "must have been recomputed … thus causing the loss of about ten turns of the hand".

The accents count the numbers a variable has held, and name numbers, not columns: No. 2's v₁″ = v₁ = a is the a that comes back into v1 from its complement in v8.

The manuscript is BAB/L/001 in the Babbage Papers, Science Museum Group (catalogue and scans), read from the scans. The transcription has not been checked by a person, and nothing here is claimed as new. Source: analytical-engine.
